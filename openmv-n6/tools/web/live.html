How AI layers and person locks work
student evidence reveals the intermediate T/R/TR boxes and rejected static-candidate ticks for model debugging. It is hidden by default; inference, fusion and person lock keep running. The sliders hide candidates, they do not stop the engines: the count beside each channel reads shown of found, so a quiet scene and a slider parked too high never look the same. They cannot go below the threshold the engines were started with (−−student-conf).
lock keeps a person between frames instead of deciding again every frame: seen twice, they get an id (P1) and are held through the frames no sensor found them in, for up to 1.5 s, carried on their own velocity. A held box is amber and dashed and says coast 0.4s — it is where somebody probably is, not where anybody saw them. The letters after the id are the sensors confirming it right now: D detector, T thermal, R radar, F fusion. A channel switched off still feeds the lock — hiding a channel hides its boxes, it does not make the viewer forget what it saw.
a candidate the detector has never seen has to MOVE before it is drawn as a person. In the lobby this rig sits in, a lit glass door reads 31.7°C and a person reads 30.9°C — the same temperature, so nothing radiometric can tell them apart, and a door has never moved. Those candidates are counted as static beside the lock rather than dropped silently: something warm and motionless is not nobody.
with thermal outline on, the thermal channel draws a smoothed warm boundary, and fusion draws a ring around it. This is a thermal estimate, not a person segmentation mask; warm furniture may overlap a person. Partial, fragmented or poorly supported shapes fall back to a rectangle: a full rectangle above 0.75, four corner ticks below. orange = thermal student, cyan = radar student, white = the same person in both. The pair is made on the horizontal only (±50 px): radar elevation comes from two elements and says almost nothing about which box a return belongs to. A fused box carries the range, which is the one thing the picture cannot show, and its components keep their colour but drop their own labels.
fusion keeps running the channel it needs even when that channel's own boxes are switched off — switching one off hides it, agreement still needs both.
How to read this page

The trust bar is the only thing to check before writing a number down. Amber means the readings are usable but qualified — the qualification changes how to read them, it is not a nag to clear. Red means do not record anything. All green is not a claim that the measurement is accurate; it is a claim that none of the failures this code can see are happening.

Hover readings, pinned probes, and the delta

Hover for a reading. The number comes from the thermal frame behind that pixel — before the AGC and before the guided filter, which borrows the visible camera's edges to sharpen the picture and must not be quoted off. Absolute accuracy is ±5°C at best. The board runs the Lepton in high gain since 2026-08-09; before that it was silently in low gain, whose specified accuracy is the greater of ±10°C or 10%, so any reading quoted off a frame recorded earlier than that carries the looser number. The Δ between two pinned points of the same material in one frame is far better than either, and is what a finding should rest on — provided neither point is clipped, neither sits on a rebuilt row, and no shutter event separates them. Click the image to pin a probe; readings keep working in every view.

Judging registration

The fused view cannot tell you whether the warp is right: the guided filter puts crisp edges in the right places even when the thermal layer is offset, so a misregistered frame still looks sharp — it just colours the wrong side of the edge. Use blink (the eye catches motion far better than offset), mix to judge how far off it is, or edges, which draws the thermal layer's own edges over the plain visible image: where the warp is right they land on the object's outline. thermal footprint outlines where the thermal camera stops seeing at all — outside it the grey is not a cold reading, it is no reading.

Operator view

operator keeps visible luminance and uses the registered thermal layer mainly for colour. Its weight adapts to local visible contrast and the footprint fades into plain grey at the edge. It is easier to watch than fused, but its rendered colour is not a temperature scale; use the probe and detection labels for measurements.

The clock

One thermal period drawn to scale, with the moment the visible frame was actually grabbed marked inside it. Both timestamps come off the board's own clock; arrival times on this host have been through a 4KB-chunked CDC write, a 500ms stall retry and the host's scheduler, and measure the link rather than the sensors. The grey band at the right is the spread of the thermal interval, which on this part is a three-valued delta function (113/114/115 ms) — so a wide band there is a real anomaly, not ordinary jitter.
